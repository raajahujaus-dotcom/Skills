# Resume Structure

This skill is the formatting layer for a resume. The content always comes from the user's real experience. Never invent or alter employers, titles, dates, or metrics to fit a formatting rule.

## Collect first

Ask for anything missing before building:

- Name and contact details
- Roles with employers, titles, and accurate dates
- Metrics and outcomes the user can stand behind
- The target job description, if tailoring
- Total years of experience

## Page layout

These are defaults. If the user has a template they like, follow it.

```
PAGES:        2 for roughly 7+ years of experience, 1 for early career
FONT:         Calibri
BODY TEXT:    10pt
NAME:         22pt, centered, bold
CONTACT:      9pt, centered, single line
SECTION HEAD: 11pt, bold, bottom border line
ROLE TITLE:   10pt, bold italic
DATES:        10pt, right-aligned, same line as company
MARGINS:      0.7" all sides
LINE SPACING: 1.0 (single)
```

## Section order

Recruiters often scan the top third of page one first, so put the strongest evidence there.

```
PAGE 1:
1. NAME
2. CONTACT LINE
3. PROFESSIONAL SUMMARY (3-4 lines)
4. KEY ACHIEVEMENTS (3-4 bold-metric bullets)
5. PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE (continues to page 2)

PAGE 2:
5. PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE (continued)
6. CORE COMPETENCIES (pipe-separated keywords from the job description)
7. EDUCATION & CERTIFICATIONS
```

## Date formatting

Pick one consistent style and apply it to every role. A common convention:

| Situation | Format | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Role spans multiple calendar years | Year only | 2020 – 2022 |
| Role within one calendar year, or under 12 months | Month/Year | Jun 2024 – Oct 2024 |
| Current role | Month/Year – Present | Nov 2025 – Present |

Dates must always be accurate. Formatting never justifies changing them.

## Bullet counts

Scale bullets with tenure so each role's space matches the time spent there.

| Tenure | Bullets |
|---|---|
| 2+ years | 4-5 |
| 1-2 years | 3-4 |
| 6-12 months | 3 |
| Under 6 months | 2-3 |

## Bullet format

For sales and other metric-driven roles, lead with the number. Recruiters scan for numbers first.

**Metric-first (preferred when the role is quantifiable):**
> $2.4M enterprise portfolio (+35% YoY) built through account planning and multi-product expansion

This example is illustrative. Use only the user's real figures.

**XYZ backup (when metric-first does not read well):**
> Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]

Rules:

- Every bullet contains at least one metric ($, %, or a count). If the user has none, ask for an estimate they can defend, or use a concrete scope (team size, number of accounts).
- No two bullets in the same role cover the same theme.
- Avoid filler verbs such as "spearheaded," "leveraged," "orchestrated," and "synergized."
- If a bullet could appear on anyone's resume in the same field, it is too generic. Rewrite it.
- Read each bullet aloud. If it sounds like a press release, cut the filler.

## ATS-safe file rules

- Default to **.docx** for applicant-tracking portals, since it parses most reliably. Follow the employer's instructions if they ask for another format.
- Also produce a **.pdf** for direct sends such as email or messages.
- No tables, columns, text boxes, graphics, or content in headers and footers.
- Standard bullets (•) only, and a pipe (|) to separate competencies.
- Avoid special characters beyond • | and the em dash.
- File naming: `Resume_[First]_[Last]_[Company]_[Role]_[MonthYear].docx`

## Keyword optimization

Before delivering:

1. Extract the hard skills, tools, and role-specific terms from the job description.
2. Check each one against the resume.
3. Aim for strong coverage of the keywords the user can truthfully claim. Never add a skill they do not have.
4. Place the highest-priority keywords in the summary, the key achievements, and the bullets.
5. Use the Core Competencies section for remaining keywords that did not fit naturally.

## Self-employed and founder roles

Use a title that accurately describes the work. If the user ran a business or consulted independently, describe the client outcomes and applied skills, with two or three bullets. Do not hide a role that is relevant, and do not inflate or disguise it.

## Professional summary

Three or four lines that include:

- Years of experience and a recognizable anchor employer or domain
- One headline metric
- Target-role keywords from the job description
- One differentiator

Structure:
> [Role] with [X] years of experience across [verticals or company types]. [Headline metric]. Combines [keyword 1] and [keyword 2] with [differentiator].

## Key achievements section

Three or four bullets. Each should stand alone as a proof point a recruiter can read in isolation. Pull the single best metric from each of the top roles, bold the metric, and keep each to one line.

Format:
- **[Metric]** [outcome] at [employer]

## Quality checklist

Run this before delivering:

- [ ] Is it the right length (2 pages, or 1 for early career)?
- [ ] Is body text 10pt Calibri (or the user's chosen template)?
- [ ] Are dates right-aligned, accurate, and formatted consistently?
- [ ] Does every bullet have at least one metric or concrete scope?
- [ ] Are there no duplicate themes within a role?
- [ ] Does the top third of page 1 contain at least three hard metrics?
- [ ] Is the ATS version a .docx?
- [ ] Does the keyword check cover the job description's key terms the user can claim?
- [ ] Are all titles, dates, and metrics accurate?
- [ ] Would a recruiter scanning for six seconds know the role and the fit?

## A note on sources

These defaults reflect widely shared recruiter and ATS guidance. If you cite a statistic from any study, verify it first.
